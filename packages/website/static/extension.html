Get the extension
Extension
Scam links stop loading. Drainers stop before the wallet opens.

PayHole for Chrome checks every site you open against the public PayHole resolver, the same answer the Android app gets, and puts a wall in front of wallet drainers, phishing pages, and counterfeit token sites before they load. It also reads what a page asks your wallet to sign and stops a send, an approval, or a permit to a known drainer address before the wallet opens. It runs in Chrome, Brave, Edge, Arc, Opera, and Vivaldi. Until the Chrome Web Store listing is approved it installs from a zip in about two minutes.

Download payhole-extension.zipCheck a link
Version 0.3.0. Manifest V3. No account, no sign-up. The only thing that leaves the browser is the name of each site, sent once to the resolver, which keeps no log.
1. Install it

Two minutes. The only unusual step is loading the folder instead of clicking an install button, which is how every extension outside the store is installed.

01
Download and extract
Download the zip and extract it into a folder you will keep, for example Documents/payhole-extension. On Windows right-click the zip and choose Extract All; on a Mac double-click it. The browser loads the extension from this folder on every start, so do not delete it afterwards. Inside you should see manifest.json and a few folders.
02
Load it unpacked
Open chrome://extensions in Chrome, brave://extensions in Brave, or edge://extensions in Edge. Turn on Developer mode at the top right, click Load unpacked, and pick the folder you extracted. Do not pick a subfolder. PayHole appears in the list with its green vortex icon.
03
Pin it
Click the puzzle icon right of the address bar and pin PayHole so the icon stays visible. To update later, download the zip again, extract it over the same folder, and click the small refresh arrow on the PayHole card in chrome://extensions. Your settings stay.
2. What it does

Nothing to set up. From the moment it is pinned, every site you open is checked, and every wallet request is read before the wallet sees it.

04
The wall
Open a listed name and the page never loads. In its place: what the name is, who says so, and two ways out. Go back, or Open once for ten minutes. A name the browser already saw blocked is caught by a rule inside the browser itself, before a single request leaves, even while the extension is asleep.
05
The badge and the popup
The icon carries a red mark on a blocked tab. Click it on any site for that site's verdict, a Report button, and a box to check any link you were sent before you open it.
06
Report
Not on any list, but you know better? Report it from the popup: pick wallet drainer, phishing, counterfeit token, or drainer infrastructure, add a note, send. The resolver gathers evidence on every report.
07
Right-click any link
Check this link with PayHole in the context menu opens the verdict without visiting the site. The same entry works on the page you are on.
08
Blocked by mistake?
Open once lets this browser through for ten minutes. Shared platforms stay reachable through the resolver's allowlist. Every wall links to the same check on payhole.org, where the sources are listed. The Shield tab of the dashboard shows everything stopped this session and lets you turn the shield off.
09
The wallet guard
Before MetaMask, Rabby, or any wallet opens, the extension reads what the page asked for: a send, an approval, a permit, a signature. Every address in it is checked against the network's list of drainer contracts and the wallets behind them, ScamSniffer's list plus what the project confirmed. A known one is stopped with a warning you can override; an unlimited approval to an address nobody knows gets a heads-up. Works with every wallet, on every chain, and never touches a seed.
10
Reports that pay
The browser now has a reporter key of its own, like the phone. Every report is signed by it, and the Reports tab of the dashboard takes the wallet bounties go to, the proof from the link page that ties the key to a tier holder, and shows what became of each report. Addresses can be reported too: paste one in the popup's check box. The rules are the same as the app's: 0.50 USDG for a drainer contract or its infrastructure, 0.30 for a phishing page or wallet, first confirmed report only.
3. The pocket, if you want it

The extension also carries a capped USDG pocket that pays websites, tools, and agents over x402 while you browse. It is off by default and the shield needs none of it. Turn it on in the dashboard's Shield tab, under Pay module, and the Budget, Sites, Agents, Registry, Tips, and Tiers tabs appear.

11
Create a seed
Open the Budget tab. Enter a password twice and click Create. The recovery phrase is shown once. Write it down on paper and keep it somewhere safe: anyone with the phrase controls every address of this pocket, and without it a lost browser means lost access. Already have a PayHole seed from another browser? Choose Import a seed instead and paste the phrase.
12
Copy the owner address
The Budget tab shows Owner with a Copy button, plus Owner ETH and Owner USDG. Send to that address on Robinhood Chain, chain id 4663: some USDG to start with, 1 to 5 is plenty, and a little ETH for gas, 0.001 ETH covers many transactions on this chain. Until the ETH arrives the panel says "needs a little ETH for gas".
13
Create the account and top up
When both balances show, click Create account: one transaction from the owner, and the BudgetAccount line changes to the pocket's address. Then enter an amount in the Top up panel, for example 5, and click Top up. Two transactions move USDG from the owner into the pocket. A note about a skipped fee is normal until the token's swap route exists; the fee is what buys and burns PAYHOLE on every top-up once it does.
14
Pay for a page
Open payhole.org/try.html and click Load the article. The page asks for 0.01 USDG with a 402. The first time you pay a site, the owner sends two transactions: one sets the site's cap on chain, one funds the site's own address with a small chunk from the pocket, 0.5 USDG by default. Then the extension signs the payment, the facilitator settles it on chain, and the article appears with the settlement transaction under it. The second load pays instantly.
15
Per-site caps and the prompt
Every site gets a cap, 1 USDG by default. The Sites tab lists every site that was ever paid with its spend, cap, payment count, and last payment. Change a cap and Save, or Block a site so it is never paid again. A price above a site's cap, or a day's total above the threshold, opens the approval page instead of paying: the site, the resource, the amount, who gets paid, and what is left of each cap. Pay once approves that one request without changing any cap.
16
Pause, withdraw, lock
Pause all payments, under Pay module in the Shield tab, stops every payment until you untick it. Withdraw in the Budget tab moves USDG from the pocket back to the owner, or to any address you type. Lock pocket at the top of the dashboard locks the vault until you enter the password again. Locked means no payments; the shield keeps working either way.
4. Beyond the basics
Blocklist
Your own list
Flag a hostname yourself, export the list, and push it to a Sinkhole node on your network by entering its address and token. Your entries block in this browser and on that node, on top of everything the resolver already knows.
Agents
Session keys for tools
The Agents tab creates a named key with its own cap and hands you an environment block for the payhole command line. A script pays APIs from the same pocket, never above its cap, never holding your seed.
Tips
Pay creators per visit
Off by default. Turn it on in the Tips tab and registered sites receive a small tip once per domain per day, from a small float. The Registry tab is where a site owner claims their own domain.
Tiers
Buy a tier, burn the token
The Tiers tab shows what each tier unlocks: bigger pockets, more agent keys, flags with weight in the swarm. A tier is paid in USDG; the vault buys PAYHOLE with it and burns it. The tier page does the same from any wallet.
Settings
The resolver and the defaults
The Shield tab holds the resolver address, dns.payhole.org by default, or your own Sinkhole node. Settings holds the RPC endpoint, the contract addresses, the default site cap, the top-up chunk, and the prompt threshold. Reset clears local data; Remove seed deletes the vault from this browser.
Recovery
If the browser is lost
Install PayHole anywhere, turn the pocket on, choose Import a seed, enter the phrase. The pocket and every address come back, because they are derived from the seed and the money never left the chain.
Good to know

The shield sends one thing to the resolver: the hostname of a site, the first time you open it in a while. Not the page, not the path, not who you are. The resolver answers from memory and keeps no query log. Point the extension at your own Sinkhole node and nothing goes further than your own network.

The pocket's seed never leaves the extension, and PayHole has no server that sees it. Payments are signed transfer authorizations; the site or its facilitator settles them, and only the amount asked, within your caps. A site learns exactly one thing about you: the address that pays it.

Removing the extension deletes everything it stored in that browser. A pocket and its balance stay on chain under your seed; re-import the seed to reach them again.

Questions about what the extension can and cannot do are answered on the Trust page, and what it stores and sends is on the privacy page.
